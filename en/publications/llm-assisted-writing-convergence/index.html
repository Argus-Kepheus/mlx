Convergencia textual y estilística en la escritura asistida por grandes modelos de lenguaje: una revisión crítica de la evidencia reciente

in preparation review article

Calvimontes Calvimontes, T. de J. Convergencia textual y estilística en la escritura asistida por grandes modelos de lenguaje: una revisión crítica de la evidencia reciente [Textual and stylistic convergence in writing assisted by large language models: A critical review of recent evidence]. Manuscript in preparation for Revista Científica Humanidades (UAJMS), Vol. 8, No. 14.

This page brings together two materials that the article cannot include within its length: the verification of the figures from its three core studies and the list of the 53 works it cites. The article is written in Spanish and is a publication related to the Digital multilingualism front.

Verification of the core studies

Each figure the article takes from the three core studies is listed with its location in the published version (PDF page and section, table or figure), so that it can be checked against the source.

The check was made with AI assistance on 28 October 2026 and repeated on 18 November 2026.

Padmakumar and He (2024)

Published version (ICLR 2024)

  • 38 professional writers: p. 1 and appendix A.1, p. 14.
  • 300 essays on ten topics in three conditions (unassisted, GPT-3, InstructGPT): p. 3.
  • ROUGE-L between the key points of essays on the same topic: 0.1536 unassisted, 0.1578 with GPT-3 and 0.1660 with InstructGPT: § 4.2, p. 5, and table 10, p. 22.
  • Significant difference with InstructGPT (p < 0.05), not with GPT-3: § 4.2, p. 5.
  • The InstructGPT result holds with BERTScore and with whole essays: § 4.2, p. 5, and table 10, p. 22.
  • The repetition comes from text taken from the model: figure 4(b), p. 8.

Agarwal et al. (2025)

Published version (ACM)

  • 118 participants and 472 essays: p. 7.
  • Increase in similarity within each group: India, t(3622) = −7.22, d = −0.26; United States, t(3246) = −9.45, d = −0.33; p < 0.001: p. 9.
  • Difference-in-differences between the two increases: p = 0.30; 95% CI [−0.018, 0.006]: p. 9.
  • Similarity between the two groups: from 0.48 unassisted to 0.54 assisted: p. 9, figure 6(b).
  • 1,104 similarities = 4 topics × 276 pairs of 24 participants (Indian group, unassisted): p. 9.

Baumler et al. (2026)

Published version (ACL Anthology). PDF pages; p. 1 is p. 43867 of the proceedings.

  • Pre-registered study with 81 participants: p. 1.
  • LUAR, chosen for its authorship-identification performance on the sample's control texts: § 5.2, p. 5, and appendix B.2.
  • Similarity to the participant's own text after post-editing: g = 0.55; 95% CI [0.38, 0.71] (H1a): p. 5.
  • Similarity to the model output: g = −0.41 (H1b): p. 5.
  • Closer to the model's style than to their own: g = −1.43; 95% CI [−1.55, −1.32] (H1c): p. 6.
  • Homogeneity across authors compared with unassisted texts: g = 1.42; 95% CI [1.33, 1.51] (H2a); compared with generated drafts: g = −0.69 (H2b): p. 6.
  • With CISR: g = 1.03 (H2a) and −0.66 (H2b): table 3, p. 16.

Works cited

The 53 works are grouped by their role in the article. 45 were examined in full text; the 8 marked "abstract only" were consulted through their abstract, and what the article says about them is limited to it.

The list comes from reconciling three working inventories: the bibliography of the expanded draft (53 records), a bibliographic matrix (47) and an earlier bibliography (46), totalling 146 records of 80 works. The 27 works not incorporated are candidates the expanded draft did not cite; they were not excluded by a criterion or by the direction of their results.
